Eight Reasons to Turn Off the Highway

A river, an island, a cave, a sandbar, a church, a pool, a sitio and a baywalk. All eight photographed where they actually are, with no borrowed scenery.

Published 2026-08-23

The coastal highway does its job well, which is the problem. It moves you past Masinloc efficiently enough that the town reads as a stretch of road with a church on it. Everything below requires a deliberate turn, and most of the turns are not signposted in any way a stranger would notice.

There are eight places documented on this site with their own photographs, taken where they are, of what they are. That last part sounds like a low bar. It is not: a great deal of Philippine destination imagery online is borrowed from somewhere more photogenic, and a visitor who arrives expecting the borrowed version leaves disappointed in the real one.

Inland and along the water

Hamat River and Bunga Cave are the inland pair, reached by turning away from the sea rather than towards it. Coto Kidz Pool is what its name says. Bacala Sandbar appears and disappears with the tide, which is either the appeal or the frustration depending on your timing. Sitio Buri and the Masinloc Baywalk are where the town meets its own coastline, the Baywalk in particular being the place the town goes rather than the place it sends visitors.

San Salvador Island is the one with the deepest documented history — it sits inside the protected marine area, and it turns up by name in a Spanish account of a raid in 1649. San Andres Church is the one everybody sees and few people look at properly, which is why it has an article of its own.

None of these places were built for visitors. That is the reason to go, and the reason to behave well when you do.

Practical honesty

This site does not publish transport schedules, opening hours or entrance fees, because those change without notice and a stale schedule is worse than none. What it publishes is where the places are, what they look like, and what is documented about them. Ask locally for the current details, which is in any case how you find out anything useful in a town this size.
