The Sambal Words We Refuse to Lose

Sambal Tina is not a dialect of Tagalog. It is its own language, it is spoken here, and a good deal of it currently lives in the memory of people over sixty.

Published 2026-08-23

The first thing to establish, because it is the thing most often got wrong from outside: Sambal is a language, not a dialect of Tagalog. It belongs to the Austronesian family, as Tagalog and Ilocano do, but it is a sibling rather than a variant. Speaking Sambal is not speaking Tagalog with an accent.

The variety spoken in Masinloc is the one this project records as Sambal Tina. Both Tagalog and Ilocano are also spoken here, and both have left marks on how people talk, which is what happens to any language on a coast with a highway through it.

Where the language is holding

Language loss is rarely total and rarely even. It happens by domain. A language can disappear from schoolwork and official business while holding firmly in the kitchen, on a boat, at a funeral, and in the names of plants, fish and shellfish. That is roughly the shape of it here. The words for what you catch, cook and grow are among the most durable, precisely because they are used by people doing those things daily.

A dictionary nobody can correct is a monument. A dictionary anybody can correct is a language.

The dictionary on this site holds several thousand entries drawn from an existing archival source, kept deliberately separate from a second layer of speaker-confirmed usage. The separation matters: the archive records what the source said, and corrections read as corrections, attributed and dated, rather than as a silent rewrite of the record. Anyone who grew up with these words can submit an addition or a correction.

Why spelling is not a small question

Written Sambal has no single settled orthography, and the same word can appear in several spellings across sources of different ages. This project's rule is not to quietly modernise a spelling to make a page tidier. Where an older source spells something one way and current speakers say it another, both are recorded, because the difference between them is itself evidence.
