How the town makes a living

How Masinloc Makes a Living

A power station, a protected bay, mango season, and the people who left. Four economies in one municipality, none of which can be described without the others.

Published 2026-08-23

A coastal town of fifty-six thousand people does not have an economy. It has several, running at different speeds, and a household will usually be attached to more than one of them.

The plant

The coal-fired station in Barangay Bani runs three units totalling 1,023 megawatts and feeds the Luzon grid. For the municipality it is an employer of a particular kind of skilled worker, a procurement channel, a ratepayer and a permanent item on the local agenda. It is also the reason Masinloc appears in national arguments about energy that the town did not start.

The water

Fishing here happens inside a legal instrument. The bay is a protected area of more than seven and a half thousand hectares, and the rules that come with that status shape who may fish where, with what, and when. That is a real constraint on a real livelihood, and it is the reason enforcement, community management and local participation are not abstractions in this town.

The fruit

Zambales mangoes carry a reputation that reaches well beyond the province, and Masinloc has a genuine growing history within that. Mango income is seasonal and weather-exposed in the way tree-crop income always is, which makes it a supplement to household income more often than a foundation for it.

The people who left

The fourth economy does not appear in any local ledger. Masinloqueños work in Manila, on ships, and in a long list of other countries, and money moves home. This is the least visible and often the most decisive part of how a Philippine coastal town actually funds itself, and it is why the outward-facing half of this project exists at all: a town's diaspora is part of its economy, and staying findable to it is not sentiment.

A town with a diaspora has an economy with a postal address somewhere else.

What this article is not

It is not a jobs page. Current openings, employers, applicants and the workflows around them belong to Masinloc Connect, which is built for it and can keep the information accurate. Discover explains how the town earns; Connect is where the actual opportunities live. Keeping those apart is what stops this section quietly rotting into a stale listings page.
