The 1,023-Megawatt Giant in Bani

Masinloc Power Plant is not one machine and not one moment in the town's history. Its three units arrived across twenty-two years, under changing ownership, beside a protected bay.

Published 2026-08-23 · Updated 2026-08-25

The power plant enters Masinloc's skyline before its numbers enter the conversation. Three generating units stand on the coast in Barangay Bani. Read as one silhouette, they look like one project; read by operating date, they tell a twenty-two-year story of expansion and changing ownership.

The specifics, from the operator's own published plant profile rather than from local estimate: Units 1 and 2 are rated at 344 megawatts each and began operating in 1998, originally developed under the National Power Corporation. Unit 3, rated at 335 megawatts, entered commercial operation in September 2020. Together the three units come to a nameplate capacity of 1,023 megawatts, feeding the Luzon grid. The plant changed hands in March 2018, when SMC Global Power acquired it from AES Philippines.

Why the numbers are worth getting right

Large infrastructure attracts approximate description. Here, approximation hides the timeline. Units 1 and 2 use subcritical pulverized-coal technology; Unit 3 uses supercritical steam-generator technology. The operator also records a 10-megawatt battery energy storage system acquired with the plant in 2018. Coal generation and an early Philippine grid battery occupy the same industrial site.

The 1,023-megawatt total puts a barangay name inside national discussions about electricity supply and coal. It does not, by itself, disclose how many Masinloqueños work there, what the municipality receives, or what independent monitoring finds in the surrounding environment. Those are separate questions requiring separate records.

A thousand megawatts is not a local fact. It is a national one that happens to have a barangay address.

This article records the common ground every argument about the plant should start from: what is there, at what scale, since when and under whose ownership. The plant profile answers those questions. It should not be made to answer the local employment, fiscal or environmental questions that its public page does not contain.

Still open

Current employment figures, the local tax and host-community benefit arrangements, and independently published environmental monitoring for the facility are not yet documented here. Those would materially improve this article and none of them should be estimated.
