The Giant in Bani

Three units, more than a thousand megawatts, and a set of facts about the power station in Barangay Bani that are worth knowing precisely rather than approximately.

Published 2026-08-23

You can see it from the highway. In Barangay Bani, on the coast, there is a coal-fired power station that is by a wide margin the largest built structure in the municipality, and one of the larger ones on this side of Luzon. People who grew up in Masinloc measure distances against it without noticing they are doing it.

The specifics, from the operator's own published plant profile rather than from local estimate: Units 1 and 2 are rated at 344 megawatts each and began operating in 1998, originally developed under the National Power Corporation. Unit 3, rated at 335 megawatts, entered commercial operation in September 2020. Together the three units come to a nameplate capacity of 1,023 megawatts, feeding the Luzon grid. The plant changed hands in March 2018, when SMC Global Power acquired it from AES Philippines.

Why the numbers are worth getting right

Large infrastructure attracts approximate description. It becomes "the power plant", a single undifferentiated thing that is either a source of jobs or a source of grievance depending on who is talking. The units were built at different times, under different owners, to different specifications, and that history is the difference between a plant that arrived in 1998 and one that has been expanded within living memory.

It matters locally in ordinary ways. A facility of this size is an employer, a ratepayer, a procurement channel and a permanent item on the municipal agenda. It also puts Masinloc into national conversations about energy supply and coal that the town did not ask to be in and cannot easily leave.

A thousand megawatts is not a local fact. It is a national one that happens to have a barangay address.

This article does not take a position on coal. It records what is there, at what scale, since when, and under whose ownership, because those are the facts every other conversation about the plant depends on and they are surprisingly hard to find stated plainly in one place.

Still open

Current employment figures, the local tax and host-community benefit arrangements, and independently published environmental monitoring for the facility are not yet documented here. Those would materially improve this article and none of them should be estimated.
