Thirteen Barangays, One Masinloc

The 2024 census shows a municipality whose largest barangay has more than six times the population of its smallest—and whose official urban-rural labels challenge the obvious map in your head.

Published 2026-08-23 · Updated 2026-08-25

The Philippine Statistics Authority's Masinloc table contains a useful surprise. Baloganon, Bani and Inhobol are classified as urban; both North and South Poblacion are classified as rural. The labels are statistical categories, not a visitor's visual judgment—and they are a warning against inventing a barangay's character from its name.

There are thirteen: Baloganon, Bamban, Bani, Collat, Inhobol, North Poblacion, San Lorenzo, San Salvador, Santa Rita, Santo Rosario, South Poblacion, Taltal and Tapuac. Together they account for Masinloc's 56,579 residents in the 2024 census.

The population is not evenly divided

Inhobol is the largest at 10,225 residents. Baloganon follows at 7,794 and Taltal at 6,109. North Poblacion is the smallest at 1,664; Santo Rosario has 2,407 and San Salvador 2,587. Those figures are not rankings of importance. They show why a municipal average can conceal very different local scales.

Some distinctions are physical and documented. Bani contains the 1,023-megawatt power plant. San Salvador is an island barangay inside the protected seascape and appears by name in an account of a 1649 raid. San Andres Church stands in South Poblacion. Hamat River and Bunga Cave are recorded in Santa Rita; Sitio Buri is in Inhobol; Coto Kidz Pool is in Taltal.

The municipality is one total. The barangays are the evidence that the total is not one kind of place.

Why the list is worth publishing

A barangay name turns a broad claim into a checkable one. It distinguishes the power plant in Bani from the church in South Poblacion, the island community of San Salvador from the upland country of Inhobol, and an image of Hamat River from a generic Masinloc landscape.

This page stops at documented population, classification and place. It does not assign occupations, personalities or traditions to thirteen communities without local evidence. The next useful layer would be barangay-level histories made with residents, not thirteen paragraphs written from a census table.
