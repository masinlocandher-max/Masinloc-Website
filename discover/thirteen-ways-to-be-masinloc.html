Thirteen Ways to Be Masinloc

The town is thirteen barangays, and they are not interchangeable. One holds the power station, one is an island, two are the old town centre, and the rest are the reason the others work.

Published 2026-08-23

Ask somebody in Masinloc where they are from and they will not say Masinloc. They will name a barangay, and the answer will carry information that the municipal name does not: whether they grew up nearer the sea or the mountains, whether the road reaches them easily, what their family probably does for a living.

There are thirteen of them: Baloganon, Bamban, Bani, Collat, Inhobol, North Poblacion, San Lorenzo, San Salvador, Santa Rita, Santo Rosario, South Poblacion, Taltal and Tapuac. That is the whole municipality. A place of fifty-six thousand people divided thirteen ways is a place where the divisions still mean something in daily life.

The ones outsiders end up hearing about

North Poblacion and South Poblacion are the old town centre, which is where the church is, where the municipal offices are, and where somebody driving through actually passes. When a visitor says they have been to Masinloc, this is almost always what they mean.

Bani carries the power station, which makes it the barangay with the largest built structure and the most national significance per square metre in the municipality. San Salvador is an island, which makes it the barangay you cannot drive to, and the one with the longest documented history — it is named in a Spanish account of a raid in 1649 and it sits inside the protected marine area.

A municipality is an administrative fact. A barangay is where somebody is actually from.

Why the list is worth publishing

Partly because it is the sort of thing that is oddly hard to find stated plainly and correctly in one place, and partly because it is the frame every other article on this site sits inside. When something here says the mangoes come from a particular place, or the fishing happens in a particular bay, the barangay is the unit that makes the sentence specific rather than decorative.

It is also the honest answer to a question outsiders ask in the wrong shape. There is no single thing that being from Masinloc looks like. There are thirteen versions of it, and people here can tell them apart at a distance.
