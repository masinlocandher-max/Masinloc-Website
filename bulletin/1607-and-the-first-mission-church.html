1607: the founding of Masinloc and its first church

The town's first documented church was modest, temporary, and completely different from the stone landmark Masinloqueños know today.

MABAYANI 22 August 2026 5 min read Faith, Conflict & Power · part 2 of 10

In 1607, Fray Andrés del Espíritu Santo travelled north from Manila by sea and reached Masinloc with Fray Jerónimo de Cristo. The historical study of the Recollect mission identifies Andrés as founder of the new pueblo and builder of its first church.

The same account records a difficult beginning. The two missionaries spent months living on root crops and vegetables. Masinloc's documented founding was not a ceremonial scene in front of a finished stone church; it began with sea travel, scarcity, and a small mission in an inhabited coastal community.

The church that disappeared

Fray Andrés built the first house of worship from light materials. It did not last. The coral-stone San Andres Church seen today belongs to a much later construction history, traditionally beginning in 1745.

Where the evidence stands

The 1607 arrival, the identity of Fray Andrés as founder, and the first church of light materials are documented in the historical study. No verified month and day for the founding have been located.

From one town to a northern network

Masinloc later became a Recollect center for northern Zambales, described in the study as reaching from Iba to Anda. Four or more missionaries commonly resided here, and one chronicler praised Masinloc as having the province's best church and convent.

The founder's work continued north. The study credits Fray Andrés and Fray Jerónimo with establishing Casborran, now Alaminos, and Bolinao in 1609, then Balincaguin, now Mabini, and Agno in 1610. Masinloc was the first documented base in that sequence.

What the source does not name

The colonial record preserves the names of the missionaries more readily than the names of local builders, translators, food providers, guides, and families who made the settlement function. Their work should not be replaced with invented names, but neither should it disappear from the interpretation.

1607 founded the documented mission town. It did not begin human life on the Masinloc coast.

That is why the founder profile on this site uses precise language. Fray Andrés is identified as founder of the documented colonial mission town, while the older presence of Sambal and Aeta communities remains part of the history before the surviving written record.
