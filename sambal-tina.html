How the project works

Gather. Compare. Verify. Keep learning.

We gather from many places

Words can come from historical dictionaries, printed indexes, older texts, everyday speech, children's materials, teachers, elders, families, and other Masinloqueños who still know and use the language.

We verify before we present

We compare spellings, meanings, source pages, and living usage instead of treating a single transcription as the whole language. When different forms are genuinely in use, we can preserve both and explain where they came from.

The community can strengthen it

A dictionary becomes better when speakers can participate. If your family uses another spelling, you know a word we have not included, or you see something that needs correction, send it to us for review.

A quick Sambal Tina lesson

Three things worth knowing before you search.

Sambal Tina has its own history, sound system, spelling traditions, and local ways of speaking. These notes help explain why you may see more than one form.

01

Sambal, Sambalic, and Tinà Sambal

In everyday community use, Sambal can refer broadly to language and identity in Zambales, and some speakers may casually describe local forms as dialects. In formal linguistic classification, however, Tinà Sambal is recognized as a language in its own right, with ISO 639-3 code xsb, within the wider Sambalic group of related languages.

On this site, “Sambal Tina” refers to the language used in Masinloc while respecting the names and varieties speakers use for themselves.

02

Tatlong patinig: A · I · O

AIO

The 1988 English–Tina Sambal–Pilipino Dictionary describes the modern Tinà Sambal orthography as having three vowels: A, I, and O, together with fourteen consonants.

That is why Sambal Tina spelling can look different from Filipino even when a word sounds familiar.

03

C or K? You may see both.

Older Sambal Tina writing was influenced by Spanish spelling. A later orthography moved closer to Pilipino and used K. In Masinloc learning materials, you may still encounter community forms written with C, such as Bocla beside Bokla, or Nacwa beside Nakwa.

K is not automatically wrong, and C is not automatically wrong. We preserve documented variation, identify the form used in our learning materials, and avoid silently changing a source.

Reference notes: the 1988 dictionary introduction describes the A-I-O vowel system and the newer orthography; current linguistic databases list Tinà Sambal as language xsb within the Sambalic group. View the Glottolog language record.

    You do not need to be a linguist to contribute. Tell us how you, your family, or your community uses the word. Every submission is reviewed before it can affect the public dictionary.

    Who is building this

    The Mabayani Project by FMB gathers, organizes, compares, and verifies material so Sambal Tina can be easier to search, learn, and share.

    The historical layer brings together the 1988 dictionary, its main English body, its printed Tina index, page references, and later source checks. The living layer brings in forms explicitly confirmed through current Masinloc project work and community usage.

    This is not meant to close the record. It is a foundation that can improve as more speakers, families, teachers, elders, learners, and researchers contribute what they know.

    Our publication rule

    We do not automatically replace one spelling with another just because it looks newer, older, or more familiar. We record where a form came from and use review status to show how it is supported.

    What the labels mean

    User-confirmed
    A form explicitly confirmed in current Masinloc project work or living usage. It may also be archive-backed, or it may be a contemporary form with its own provenance.
    Well supported
    An archival form supported by more than one source layer or matched against the printed index. 3,241 archive entries.
    Readable
    An archival form that is clear enough to read in the source layer and remains available for further checking. 1,884 archive entries.
    Needs another look
    An entry that still benefits from another source check, speaker confirmation, or closer reading before being treated as settled. 97 entries.
