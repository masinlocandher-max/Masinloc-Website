Sambal Tina 101

A few things to know before you start.

You do not need to be a linguist to learn Sambal Tina. Start with a few simple ideas, then listen to how people actually speak it.

01

Sambal and Sambal Tina

Sambal is a broader name connected to the Sambalic languages and the Sambal people of Zambales. Sambal Tina is the local variety familiar in communities including Masinloc. In everyday conversation, some people may call it a dialect. In linguistic references, Tinà Sambal is recognized as a distinct language of its own. Both ways of talking about it reflect different contexts, but here we use Sambal Tina for the language variety we are learning and documenting in Masinloc.

02

A, I, O

Traditional Sambal Tina writing is built around three core vowels: A, I, and O. You may hear sounds that resemble E or U, especially in newer speech or borrowed words, but older Sambal Tina spelling commonly represents the core vowel system with A, I, and O.

AIO
03

C and K

You may see traditional Sambal Tina words written with C where a modern Filipino reader might expect K. The letter K is part of the Filipino alphabet and is not “wrong.” In this project, we respect established Sambal Tina spellings while also helping learners understand why C and K may appear differently across older and newer writing.

How we build it

We gather it

Words can come from everyday speech, families, teachers, elders, writers, learning materials, and community knowledge. The goal is to bring useful Sambal Tina together where people can actually find it.

We verify it

We review spellings and meanings, compare forms, and check unfamiliar or conflicting entries. Verification takes time, especially when one word may be remembered or written differently by different speakers.

We improve it together

If you know Sambal Tina, your knowledge matters. Submit a word, a phrase, an example sentence, or a correction. You do not need to check whether we already have it. Send what you know and we will sort and verify it during review.

    This dictionary belongs to the community too

    May alam kang salita? Isama natin.

    Maybe it is a word your lola always used. Maybe your family spells something differently. Maybe you know a better meaning, a phrase we should include, or an example that makes a word easier to understand. Send it to us.

    Why we ask. A written collection can only hold what somebody managed to write down. Sambal Tina is bigger than that — it lives in kitchens, in boats, at markets and in the way families talk to each other, and plenty of it has never appeared in any book. There are words this collection does not have yet simply because nobody has told us them. If you know one, you know something the record does not.

    Send it even if you are not sure. You do not need to search the whole collection first, and you do not need to be certain of the spelling. Our review will check whether the word is already here, compare the details, and decide whether it should be added, corrected, merged, or looked at further. Getting it from you is the part we cannot do ourselves.

    If your contribution is approved, you can choose to have your name included in our list. Your contact details remain private and are used only if we need to clarify your submission.

    What the labels mean

    Community-confirmed
    A form that has also been explicitly confirmed in current Masinloc project work.
    Verified
    An entry with stronger review and cross-checking behind it.
    Reviewed
    An entry that is usable, while additional community checking is still welcome.
    Review in progress
    An entry where a spelling, meaning, or other detail still deserves closer checking.
