Evidence

How we source Masinloc history

Discover Masinloc publishes local history from academic studies, official records, institutional archives, linguistic scholarship, heritage declarations, maps, and clearly identified community memory. The sources are listed here so anyone can inspect the basis for what we publish.

This directory is not complete, and it is not meant to look complete. Several questions about Masinloc's past remain open, and some documents named in the historical literature have not yet been located in a public copy. Where that is the case, the entry says so rather than pointing at a substitute.

Historical studies

Scholarly work on Masinloc and the wider Zambales coast.

  1. Academic

    Masinloc, Zambales: Augustinian Recollect Mission (1607–1902)

    Emmanuel Luis A. Romanillos

    Philippine Social Science Journal, vol. 2, no. 2, 2019, pp. 151–171

    The principal historical study behind most of what this site publishes on the Spanish colonial period in Masinloc: the 1607 arrival of the Recollect mission, the first church of light materials, the 1649 defence of the coast, the construction tradition and restoration history of the stone church, the separation of San Vicente, and the 1901–1902 transition to municipal authority. Page references used on this site fall between 151 and 171.

    • 1607
    • 1649
    • San Andres Church
    • Church history

    Public copy not yet confirmed. The journal is published in the Philippines; a verified link will be added here once located.

  2. Archive

    1891 Recollect account of the Masinloc church

    Vicente Pascual

    Nineteenth-century Recollect account, quoted and discussed in Romanillos (2019)

    The source of the tradition that construction of the present stone church began in 1745, and of the finding that the widely repeated 1798 earthquake claim is unreliable. Pascual drew partly on an older book of notable events and partly on oral tradition collected from elders, and stated that the completion date was unknown. This site has not worked from an original copy: the account is used as quoted in Romanillos.

    • 1745
    • San Andres Church
    • Earthquakes

    Original not located. Used only as quoted in Romanillos (2019).

Sambal and linguistic sources

Scholarship on the Sambalic languages and the documentation of Masinloc speech.

  1. Linguistic

    English–Tina Sambal–Pilipino Dictionary

    Sotera B. Elgincolin, Priscilla R. Elgincolin and Hella Goschnick

    Summer Institute of Linguistics, 1988

    The principal modern lexicographic reference for the language spoken in Masinloc, and itself evidence of how researchers named the language in the late twentieth century. Its title is the reason the label Tina appears in older records.

    • Sambal Tina
    • Dictionary
    • 1988

    A verified public copy has not yet been confirmed. Cited bibliographically.

  2. Linguistic

    The Sambalic Languages of Central Luzon

    Roger Stone

    Comparative linguistic study

    Used for the Sambalic language-family frame and the history of language labels. Comparative material only: forms recorded for Botolan, Bolinao or Aeta languages are not treated as Masinloc Sambal.

    • Sambal Tina
    • Linguistics

    Public copy not yet confirmed.

  3. Academic

    The Central Luzon Group of Languages

    Ronald S. Himes

    Oceanic Linguistics, vol. 51, no. 2, 2012, pp. 490–537

    Used for the classification placing Sambal within the Central Luzon group alongside Kapampangan.

    • Sambal Tina
    • Linguistics

    Journal is indexed academically; a verified public link will be added once confirmed.

  4. Archive

    Writings on Zambal grammar and glossary

    Fray Rodrigo de San Miguel

    Referenced in Romanillos (2019)

    An early missionary grammar and glossary of the Zambal language, named in the Recollect historical literature. The manuscripts have not been located. If recovered they would be the earliest known documentation of the language, and would need critical reading: missionary grammars were made for conversion and imposed foreign categories.

    • Sambal Tina
    • Research gap

    Manuscripts not located. Listed here as an open research lead.
