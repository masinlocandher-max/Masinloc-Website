Research that has not been finished. Each one is stated in the article it belongs to, and each one is an invitation: if you can close one, tell us.

  • A final account should cite the best available primary or provincial source for Salcedo's 1572 movement through Zambales, while keeping the correction that it is not a founding date.

    Was Masinloc founded in 1572? What the record actually supports

  • The 1616 phase, the late nineteenth-century roof and bell-tower changes, and the twentieth-century facade work still need confirmation against the original Recollect archive, parish records, National Museum files and dated photographs.

    The first church was not the church we see today

  • A National Cultural Treasure marker is a separate event from the declaration, and the two are easy to merge into one date. A marker unveiling in November 2012 has been mentioned to us but no National Museum, Philippine Information Agency, Philippine News Agency or Official Gazette record for it has been located. Until one is, this account carries the declaration date only. If you hold a photograph of the marker, a programme, or a parish or municipal record of the unveiling, that is the missing piece.

    A church built, damaged, and rebuilt: San Andres across the centuries

  • Recollect chronicles, parish papers, Spanish military reports and family traditions may yet recover the names of the defenders.

    1649: when six caracoas came to Masinloc

  • The earliest surviving programme, newspaper report, parish reference, Elicano document, photograph and oral testimony have yet to be traced, along with how the costume and explanation changed across the twentieth century.

    What Binabayani remembers, and what the record can confirm

  • A final explanation requires review by fluent speakers across age groups and barangays, together with a historical linguist.

    Why older sources call the language "Tina"

  • The names of the 1649 defenders, the earliest surviving reference to Binabayani, a community-approved account of the language name, dated photographs of the church facade before 1970, and a certified roster of municipal leadership all remain unrecovered.
